## LinkedIn — founder post, share to the company page

One idea: pace is a design decision, not a feeling.

```
A year ago I had an existential crisis about how fast AI was moving. Not that it was getting smart — that the gap between what it could do and what the people around me could understand was compounding.

This weekend Dario Amodei wrote "We must slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models." Sam Altman agreed. Elon Musk wrote "Dario is right."

I couldn't slow the frontier from Rhode Island. So on December 3 I formed a construction company with a software layer — Machine Learning Systems — and pointed it at the most ordinary form of wealth there is: the house a family already owns. Deconstruction in lieu of demolition. Rebuild the home from its own materials. Let the equity stay with the people who lived there.

What I learned building it: you don't slow a system down by asking nicely. You slow it down in the grammar.

So the system stores claims, not facts. Every mind claims. Five seats ground. Only a human stamps. My AI agents don't talk to each other — they talk to a ledger, and a person reads the ledger.

When I read Dario's first step — evaluators embedded inside the lab with employee-level access — I recognized it. That's what the seats are.

The labs will pace the frontier. The rest of us should pace the arrival.

Full piece: [canonical link]

(No ML Systems deconstruction has been performed yet. The software is real; the loop is modeled. I say which is which every time.)
```

```
Worth noting what "pace" means in practice for a small builder: it isn't waiting. It's deciding in the system's grammar who's allowed to say what's true. Claims not facts, humans embedded in every output, signatures that lapse when content changes. That's implementable today at any scale — I run seven agents and one ledger on it.
```
